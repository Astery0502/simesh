# INT-001 Design: Bounded M0 Execution

## Alternatives

One full-halo pass could drive every consumer, but pointwise placement and the
serial reduction would repeatedly load diagonal support they never read. Fully
fusing gather, halos, operators, sampling, and scatter would erase the proven
capability boundaries and make numerical/order failures difficult to localize.
Separate per-capability workspaces would inflate the temporary budget.

Use two passes with one reused padded payload, one ID vector, one reused
one-field output workspace, and one scalar reduction state:

1. no-closure primary chunks gather selected fields, compute/scatter the
   pointwise result, place the exact native grid, zero-order sample, and update
   the persistent reduction;
2. full-halo chunks gather interiors, apply HAL-001/HAL-002, compute/scatter the
   central derivative, and trilinearly sample completed primaries.

All final arrays are caller-owned results and sit outside the temporary budget;
the read-only source and topology/maps are external inputs. Capacity is derived
from exact bytes per slot and must fit the topology's maximum one-block closure.
The executor preflights every lower capability with zero-slot views before any
result mutation, then runs the established primitives without a new numerical
kernel or dynamic dispatch system.

Independent review showed that zero-slot calls alone cannot validate semantic
topology, selected-ID map consistency, closure, or trilinear endpoint stencils.
The corrected design validates the complete face table against MOR coordinates
without scratch, computes capacity/closure, allocates the budgeted workspaces,
dry-plans both passes with actual IDs, and invokes the existing selected
geometry/closure/stencil validators on every planned chunk. Only after that dry
traversal may result mutation begin.

## Optimization Result

Capacity measurements show why the two-pass design is retained. On the
`8x6x4`, `8^3` benchmark, capacity 27 incurs 10.25x full-halo load amplification
and about 57 ms warm runtime; capacities 64, 128, and 192 reduce this to 3.16x/
17.6 ms, 1.78x/12.1 ms, and 1.0x/10.4 ms. Pass two dominates, especially
physical halos, sibling halos, and trilinear sampling. Full fusion would erase
the tested boundaries, while increasing capacity already captures the useful
call/support reuse. The executor therefore keeps separate primitives and exposes
the exact budget/capacity trade-off.
