# SAM-001 Evidence

## Correctness And Composition

Seven focused tests and all 94 accumulated rewrite tests pass. The tests cover
padded and unpadded sources, asymmetric root/block shapes, reordered/repeated/
partial/empty selections, deterministic later-slot duplicate wins, exact special
binary64 representations, unselected-output preservation, source preservation,
and atomic region/output/ID/map/readonly/overlap failures.

A non-cubic `3x2x4` root with `2x4x2` blocks and three fields agrees bitwise
with current `uniform_full_level1` over the entire field-first native grid. The
placement uses only integer block coordinates and cell boxes; no physical float
comparison participates.

The bounded integration traverses a `5x3x2` topology with a reusable seven-slot
workspace, gathers fields in reordered local order through STO-001, and places
only each STO-002 primary prefix. Every global block is covered exactly once,
the workspace object is reused, and the completed grid is bitwise equal to
independent direct placement.

Independent implementation audit ran 1,000 cases over random nonsymmetric
root/block shapes, zero to three fields, empty/partial/reordered/repeated
selections, asymmetric padded source boxes, and arbitrary `uint64` payload and
output patterns. Every result agreed bitwise with an independent oracle,
including duplicate order and preservation guarantees. The audit found no
correctness, validation, composition, allocation, export, or benchmark defect.

## Runtime, Throughput, And Allocation

The retained benchmark uses a `16x12x8` root, `8^3` blocks, and 15 repeats for
direct placement:

| Fields | Selected | Order | Runtime | Mvalue/s | Read+write GB/s |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 64 | Morton | 36.75 us | 891.6 | 14.27 |
| 1 | 64 | shuffled | 34.67 us | 945.2 | 15.12 |
| 1 | 512 | Morton | 128.37 us | 2,042.0 | 32.67 |
| 1 | 512 | shuffled | 128.75 us | 2,036.1 | 32.58 |
| 4 | 64 | Morton | 77.96 us | 1,681.3 | 26.90 |
| 4 | 64 | shuffled | 70.37 us | 1,862.5 | 29.80 |
| 4 | 512 | Morton | 438.67 us | 2,390.4 | 38.25 |
| 4 | 512 | shuffled | 813.96 us | 1,288.2 | 20.61 |

Small-selection order differences are timing noise; the large four-field case
shows the expected locality advantage of Morton-order traversal. Every direct
case retains 64 traced bytes and has the same 1,624-byte traced peak. The kernel
uses contiguous-z `memcpy` rows and fixed scalar metadata only.

For a full four-field `16x12x8` reconstruction, capacity 64 uses 24 chunks,
1,049,088 managed workspace bytes, and 4.354 ms (722.6 Mvalue/s). Capacity 256
uses six chunks, 4,196,352 managed bytes, and 3.908 ms (805.0 Mvalue/s). Both are
bitwise equal to one-shot placement. Their traced peaks are 4,888 and 2,728
bytes; the full uniform output is the requested result and is separate from the
bounded input workspace.

On an `8x6x4` current comparison with two `8^3` fields, rewrite placement takes
96.9 us versus 105.3 us current and agrees bitwise. On the small independent
reference case, NumPy takes 64.4 us and compiled placement 21.2 us (3.04x).
The retained kernel already reaches 38.25 GB/s, so parallel dispatch or special
plane/slab cases do not improve the current runtime-memory-complexity trade-off;
the measured Morton locality is preserved instead.
