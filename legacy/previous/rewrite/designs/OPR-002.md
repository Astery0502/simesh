# OPR-002 Design: Axis Central Difference

## Alternatives

The current derivative backend batches arbitrary output/field/axis/coefficient
terms and accumulates them. Copying that interface would introduce a miniature
operator language before one stencil contract is established. A conservative
all-axis reach simplifies loops but overstates halo requirements. In-place or
halo-fused execution obscures output validity and storage ownership.

Use one concrete centered first derivative along one explicit axis. The caller
supplies a source-coordinate output box, global spacing triplet, source/output
field positions, and translated destination. Required reach is one cell only on
the chosen axis and zero on the other axes. The first Cython form fixes current's
subtract then multiply arithmetic and uses a separate destination buffer.

Bounded evidence composes full-halo planning, gather, HAL-001/HAL-002, derivative
over primary interiors, and scatter. Measure that separate path before
considering multi-term fusion or in-place outputs.

Current behavior recovery confirmed that its batching layer additionally
multiplies by a coefficient and accumulates into initialized `+0.0`. Ordinary
one-term coefficient-one values agree, but that extra addition turns a central
`-0.0` into `+0.0`. OPR-002 keeps the simpler stencil result and records the
signed-zero batching difference rather than importing term accumulation.

## Optimization Result

The retained kernel uses prevalidated bounds, one branch on a call-constant
axis, and separately rounded inverse/subtract/multiply operations. All three
axes sustain roughly 13.6--14.9 GB/s effective traffic and are close to the
matching two-pass NumPy reference. The bounded capacity-256 path produces about
54 Mvalue/s including planning, gather, both halo stages, compute, and scatter.
Multi-term batching, in-place output, and halo fusion would couple otherwise
independent contracts without evidence of a material one-stencil gain, so they
are not retained.
